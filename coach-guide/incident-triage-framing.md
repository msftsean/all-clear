# All Clear Incident-Triage Framing

## 60-second opening

> “All Clear helps a response team turn a noisy moment into a shared, safe picture. A **signal** is something that may matter. A **report** is what a person or system says happened. An **incident** is the coordinated record we open when the evidence and impact call for action.
>
> Today we will practice surge triage: preserving the source, checking for duplicates, estimating magnitude, assigning severity, and meeting the right response SLA. ClearBoard makes the work visible so the next person can act without starting over.
>
> The tool supports human judgment; it does not replace emergency services or designated responders. When safety is at stake, we escalate through the real procedure first.”

## Framing notes

- **Start with evidence.** A signal may be noisy, incomplete, or duplicated. Keep it visible and describe confidence rather than treating it as fact.
- **Keep the nouns distinct.** Signals are observations; reports are submitted accounts; incidents are managed response records. A report can corroborate a signal, and several reports can belong to one incident.
- **Make magnitude concrete.** Record scope, location, time window, affected people or services, and confidence. Avoid unsupported impact estimates.
- **Severity drives the response.** Facilitate the group toward the published severity definitions and SLA targets. The highest severity credible evidence should guide the initial response.
- **ClearBoard is the shared handoff.** Every material decision needs an owner, status, timestamp, and next update time.
- **Voice and phone are intake paths.** Confirm critical details back to the caller, provide an accessible text alternative, and create the same auditable record as text intake.
- **Safety overrides pace.** Stop the exercise and follow the organization’s emergency protocol for imminent risk. Do not ask participants to role-play real trauma or sensitive personal details.

## Demonstration prompt

Use an approved fictional scenario: a monitoring signal and two independent reports indicate a rapidly growing service disruption at one location. Invite the group to merge evidence, create one incident, set an initial severity, state its SLA, assign an owner, and publish the next update on ClearBoard.
