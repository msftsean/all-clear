# All Clear Facilitator Talking Points

## Opening

**Key message:** All Clear turns fragmented observations into a coordinated, accountable response.

- Signals, reports, and incidents are related but not interchangeable.
- Start with evidence and confidence; avoid making a conclusion sound certain too early.
- The exercise uses fictional scenarios. Real safety concerns always follow the organization’s emergency procedure.

## Surge triage transition

**Key message:** In a surge, speed comes from disciplined sorting—not from skipping verification.

- Link duplicates instead of creating competing records.
- Preserve the source and timestamp of every material input.
- Record magnitude as scope, location, time window, and affected services or people.

## Severity and SLA transition

**Key message:** Severity selects the response posture; an SLA makes the next communication commitment visible.

- Use the locally approved severity definitions.
- State why the current evidence supports the level selected.
- Reassess severity as credible evidence changes.
- An SLA is measured from acknowledgement and assignment, not from a perfect diagnosis.

## ClearBoard transition

**Key message:** ClearBoard is the operational handoff, not a decorative dashboard.

- Every active incident needs an owner, current status, and next update time.
- Write concise updates that distinguish confirmed facts from pending verification.
- Close or merge duplicates with an auditable link.

## Voice and phone transition

**Key message:** Accessible intake must create the same traceable response record.

- Confirm location, callback method, and immediate safety information before summarizing.
- Offer a text alternative and do not require speech to receive support.
- Do not expose internal notes or sensitive details in a spoken update.

## Closing

**Key message:** A useful triage practice ends with a decision, a responsible owner, and a safe next action.
