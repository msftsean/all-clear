# All Clear Incident-Triage Facilitation Guide

Use this guide to run a practical All Clear walkthrough. The outcome is not a polished product demo: every participant should be able to recognize a signal, create or review an incident, explain its magnitude and severity, and use ClearBoard to coordinate the next safe action.

## Before participants arrive

### Room and access

- [ ] Projector, audio, and facilitator browser profile tested
- [ ] Shared demo workspace is signed in and ClearBoard opens
- [ ] Participants have the exercise URL and accessibility accommodations
- [ ] A visible timer and a private channel for support are available
- [ ] Headsets are available for the voice/phone segment; captions are enabled

### Demo pre-flight

- [ ] Start from a clean demo workspace with no real operational data
- [ ] Verify that a signal can be triaged into an incident
- [ ] Verify that an incident produces a report with its source and timestamp
- [ ] Confirm severity, SLA, owner, and status are visible on ClearBoard
- [ ] Test the safety escalation path with an approved fictional scenario
- [ ] Test text, voice, and phone fallback paths; never use live emergency numbers

## 90-minute run of show

| Time | Block | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Frame the exercise | Establish safety, scope, and vocabulary |
| 0:10–0:25 | Live surge triage | Separate signals, incidents, and reports |
| 0:25–0:40 | Severity and SLA | Explain magnitude, urgency, ownership, and response targets |
| 0:40–1:00 | ClearBoard practice | Assign, update, and hand off a simulated incident |
| 1:00–1:15 | Voice and phone | Demonstrate accessible intake and confirmation |
| 1:15–1:25 | Safety rehearsal | Escalate a safety-critical report without improvising |
| 1:25–1:30 | Debrief | Capture decisions, gaps, and next actions |

### Live surge triage

Start with a burst of fictional inputs: duplicate reports, an unverified sensor signal, a verified safety report, and a low-impact service request. Ask the room to state:

1. Is this a **signal**, an **incident**, or a **report**?
2. What evidence is known, missing, or duplicated?
3. What is the magnitude: scope, affected people or services, location, and time?
4. What is the next safe action, and who owns it?

Do not turn every signal into an incident. Link corroborating reports, preserve uncertainty, and keep the original source visible.

### Severity and SLA

Use the agreed local policy, not intuition. Demonstrate a simple scale:

| Severity | Meaning | Response target |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Immediate threat to life, safety, or critical operations | Acknowledge and escalate immediately |
| S2 | Significant disruption or credible risk | Acknowledge within 15 minutes; assign an owner |
| S3 | Contained impact requiring coordinated follow-up | Acknowledge within 4 hours |
| S4 | Informational, duplicate, or planned follow-up | Review in the next operating cycle |

Clarify that an SLA is a communication and response commitment, not a promise that the incident will be resolved by that time.

### ClearBoard practice

Have participants make one complete, auditable update:

- open the incident record from the relevant signal or report;
- set severity and explain the evidence behind it;
- add magnitude and affected scope without speculation;
- assign an accountable owner and response deadline;
- post a concise status update, then hand off to another participant.

## Facilitation interventions

**Green — productive analysis:** participants distinguish facts from assumptions and check the board before acting. Observe and ask, “What evidence would change your severity?”

**Yellow — drift or duplication:** participants create parallel incidents or debate labels without a next action. Ask them to link the records and name an owner.

**Red — safety-critical content:** pause the exercise, follow the organization’s emergency procedure, and involve the designated human responder. Do not diagnose, promise confidentiality, or use the demonstration system as an emergency service.

## Contingencies

- **ClearBoard unavailable:** use a projected static board and have participants narrate the fields they would update.
- **Voice unavailable:** continue with text and show the confirmation script; accessibility and auditability remain the lesson.
- **Phone unavailable:** use a recorded fictional transcript. Never place a live call as part of the exercise.
- **Running behind:** protect surge triage, severity/SLA, and safety rehearsal. Combine the debrief with the ClearBoard handoff.

## After the session

- [ ] Remove fictional exercise data from shared workspaces
- [ ] Record unresolved policy questions and owner assignments
- [ ] Share the safety procedure and accessible follow-up materials
- [ ] Send participants a short summary of the triage decisions rehearsed
