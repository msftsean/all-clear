# All Clear Incident-Triage Practice Rubric

Use this rubric for feedback during the facilitated exercise. It evaluates safe, explainable triage rather than speed alone.

| Criterion | Strong practice | Developing practice |
|---|---|---|
| Signal, report, incident distinction | Correctly classifies inputs and links related records | Treats every input as a separate incident |
| Evidence and magnitude | Preserves source, time, confidence, scope, and impact | States unsupported conclusions or omits scope |
| Severity and SLA | Selects a policy-aligned severity and names the response target | Uses severity labels without rationale or deadline |
| ClearBoard handoff | Includes owner, status, next update, and an auditable note | Leaves ownership or next action unclear |
| Safety | Escalates promptly through the human procedure and minimizes sensitive data | Continues the exercise or improvises a response |
| Voice and phone | Confirms essentials, offers accessible alternatives, and records intake consistently | Loses information or creates a separate, unauditable path |

## Feedback prompts

- What evidence supports this severity?
- What is the current magnitude, and what remains uncertain?
- Who owns the next action, and when will ClearBoard be updated?
- If this arrives by voice or phone, what must be confirmed before the call ends?
- What safety procedure overrides the normal triage flow?

## Successful demonstration

A strong demonstration uses an approved fictional surge to show a signal and reports becoming one managed incident; explains magnitude, severity, and SLA; updates ClearBoard with a named owner and next action; and clearly states the human safety escalation procedure.
